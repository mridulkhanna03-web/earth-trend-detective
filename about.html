Is a place on Earth really changing, or is it just noise? Tested on real NASA data, 2002 to 2025.

About this project

AETHER tests whether a place on Earth is really changing, or whether the change could be chance. Our entry for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, "Be An Earth System Trend Detective!". It is a static website: the NASA data is packed into one small file and your browser does every calculation. No server, no account.

One real answer, explained

We asked the tool about haze over the Indo-Gangetic Plain in India, 2003 to 2025. This is what it showed.

RISINGconfirmed

The line. Each dot is one year. The dashed line is the straight line that fits the dots best. Over 22 years the yearly average rose by 0.126 in total.

The rate. +0.057 every 10 years. The likely range is +0.041 to +0.074. Zero is not in that range, so "no change" is ruled out.

The test. If nothing were changing, a line this steep would appear less than 1 time in 1,000 (p-value below 0.001). A second test, Mann-Kendall, agrees. That is why the label says "confirmed".

How an answer is made

  1. NASA measuresSatellites and NASA models record a value for every small square of the Earth, every month.
  2. One average per regionNASA Giovanni averages each region into one value per month.
  3. One small fileWe pack six measurements for ten places into one file.
  4. You chooseA measurement, a place, a period, and the whole year or one season.
  5. One value per yearThe chosen months of each year are averaged.
  6. A line and two testsThe tool fits a straight line, then tests it with a t-test and the Mann-Kendall test.
  7. The answerRising, falling or no clear trend, with how fast and how sure.

Steps 1 to 3 are done once, before you open the app. Steps 4 to 7 run each time you change a choice.

The four answers you can get

RISINGconfirmed

The trend passes a strict test. A false alarm happens less than 5 times in 100. A falling trend is shown in blue.

RISINGborderline

The main test confirms the trend, the second test does not.

RISINGlikely, not confirmed

The ordinary test finds a trend, the strict test does not.

NO CLEAR TREND

The ups and downs are too large to tell. This does not prove that nothing is changing.

How it answers the challenge

  • What, where: the three menus and the globe.
  • Change over time: the chart, one value per year.
  • How much: the rate of change per 10 years, with its likely range.
  • Significant or not: a t-test on the trend line and the Mann-Kendall test.

The data

  • Six NASA measurements: haze and smoke (MODIS), air temperature (MERRA-2), rainfall (GPM IMERG), soil moisture (GLDAS), groundwater (GRACE), sea surface temperature (MODIS).
  • Years 2002 to 2025.
  • Downloaded from NASA Giovanni on 14 November 2026.
  • Averaged over ten places: nine regions and the whole world.

Limits

  • The 5% promise holds for records of 16 years or more. Shorter periods are less reliable.
  • Each place shows up to 30 tests. About one of them can look significant by luck.
  • A few unusual years at the end of a record can create a trend. The starting-year slider helps you check.
  • A trend is not a forecast, and the lists of common causes are general background, not results.

Words used in the app

Time series
The same measurement repeated over time, for example one value per year.
Trend
A steady rise or fall over many years, beyond the normal ups and downs.
Slope
How fast the value changes. Shown per 10 years.
p-value
The chance of seeing a trend this strong if nothing were really changing. Below 0.05 (5 in 100) counts as significant.
Significant
Unlikely to be chance. It does not mean large or important.
Likely range (confidence interval)
The span that very probably holds the true rate of change. If it includes zero, the trend is not significant.
Linear regression
The standard way to fit a straight line through points.
Mann-Kendall test
A second trend test. It only checks whether later values tend to be higher or lower, so one extreme year does not fool it.
Sen's slope
A rate of change that ignores extreme years: the middle value of all year-to-year slopes.
Aerosol optical depth (AOD)
How much haze, smoke or dust is in the air. No unit. About 0.1 is clear; above 0.4 is very hazy.
Groundwater percentile
How full the groundwater is compared with its own history: 0 is the driest on record, 100 the wettest.
Similar place
A different place whose trends point the same way. Useful for learning, not for forecasts.
Scenario
What would happen if the current rate simply continued. Not a forecast.
